excerpt: 'ABSTRACT: Using the implementation of Governmental Accounting Standards Board Statements No. 34 and 35 (GASB 34/35) across U.S. public universities and colleges, I examine how financial disclosure by research universities affects the allocation of federal research funding. I find that GASB 34/35 reduces federal research funding to public universities relative to private universities. I provide evidence that monitoring and private information signaling are likely the underlying mechanisms. Specifically, the decline is more pronounced among institutions with greater pre-reform administrative and academic complexity, and federal funding to public universities becomes more responsive to prior private gifts and state funding. GASB 34/35 also generates real effects within universities. At the output margin, patent output remains unchanged, whereas publications decline; at the efficiency margin, both patent and publication output per unit of research expenditure increase. The reform is also associated with lower faculty compensation across academic ranks. Overall, these results show that disclosure mandates for government contractors have real effects on government investment, with downstream implications for the higher education sector.'
