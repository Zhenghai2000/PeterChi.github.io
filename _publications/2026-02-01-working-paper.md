excerpt: 'ABSTRACT: Using the implementation of Governmental Accounting Standards Board Statements No. 34 and 35 (GASB 34/35) across U.S. public universities, I examine how financial disclosure affects the allocation of federal research funding. I find that GASB 34/35 reduces federal funding to public universities relative to private ones. Consistent with greater cost scrutiny, the decline is larger among institutions with higher pre-reform administrative and academic complexity, and negotiated F&A reimbursement rates also decline relatively at public universities. Furthermore, misconduct involving such research funding also declines relatively at public universities, suggesting tighter internal oversight. Analyses at the NIH project-PI level show that the GASB effects are not driven by researcher mobility or individual characteristics. Further analyses show that public universities experience a decline in the total number of publications, while both patent and publication output per unit of research expenditure increase. Overall, my results suggest that financial disclosure informs federal funding allocation and improves cost-effectiveness in the higher education sector. '
